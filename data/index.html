Third-Party Data Sources

Carbon & Place is built almost entirely from other people's data. This section lists the third-party datasets used in the analysis, what each one contributes, and where to find it.

Each dataset has two links. Version used points to the copy of the data held in one of our GitHub repos, so you can see exactly which vintage of a dataset the published results were built from. Source points to the original publisher, which is where you should go for the latest version, the full documentation, and the licence terms.

Most open datasets are republished in the inputdata repo. Some are too large for GitHub and are held on that repo's releases page, or are downloaded by the analysis pipeline when it runs. Datasets that cannot be republished, because of licensing or because access is restricted to approved researchers, are described but not held in inputdatasecure, which records the exact files used and how to obtain them.

Licence terms vary between datasets. Most are published under the Open Government Licence, but you should check with the original publisher before reusing anything listed here.

Address data: Ordnance Survey and Royal Mail

Address data is the most heavily restricted data in Carbon & Place, and the restrictions do not come from the government departments that publish the datasets. They come from Ordnance Survey and Royal Mail, whose address products sit underneath.

The property addresses shown in the Retrofit Explorer come from two places. Most come from the Energy Performance Certificate registers, which are published under the Open Government Licence. Others are matched from HM Land Registry Price Paid Data, and those carry an additional condition. Price Paid Data contains address data processed against Ordnance Survey's AddressBase Premium product, which incorporates Royal Mail's PAF database. HM Land Registry sets out the condition as follows:

Royal Mail and Ordnance Survey permit your use of Address Data in the Price Paid Data: for personal and/or non-commercial use; to display for the purpose of providing residential property price information services. If you want to use the Address Data in any other way, you must contact Royal Mail.

Carbon & Place displays this data for the purpose of providing residential property price and energy performance information, on a free, non-commercial research website. If you want to use the address data for anything else, that permission does not pass to you, and you must contact Royal Mail directly.

Two related points are worth stating plainly:

  • UPRNs are open, addresses are not. OS Open UPRN gives the location and identifier of every addressable property as open data. The address text that goes with a UPRN is a commercial product, so we publish the UPRN and the location, and the address only where it reaches us through an open register.
  • Postcode boundaries cannot be redistributed. The polygons behind the Postcode Gas & Electric layer are Code-Point Polygons, which are copyright of Royal Mail and supplied to us through Digimap for academic use. This is why postcode data is shown on the map but is not offered anywhere on this page for download.

Land Registry data and the Land Ownership Explorer

The Commercial and Corporate Ownership (CCOD) and Overseas Companies Ownership (OCOD) datasets are not open data. They are published under a Land Registry licence that permits use for research and for personal and non-commercial use only, and that requires the licensee to keep records of end users for audit and crime prevention purposes.

Because of this, the Land Ownership Explorer works differently from the rest of the site. It asks for your name before showing the map, it records visits through Google Analytics as a condition of the licence, and its data is not covered by the Open Data Commons licence that applies elsewhere. You must not scrape or bulk download it, and you cannot embed or reproduce its maps or data without written consent. The full terms are set out on the Land Ownership Explorer page, and the privacy notice explains the record keeping.

INSPIRE index polygons are published by HM Land Registry under their own conditions rather than the Open Government Licence, and the geometry remains Ordnance Survey copyright. We use them to help define building footprints and land parcels, and we do not republish them.

Personal data

Carbon & Place is built from statistics about places, not records about people, and we do not hold personal data about the people who live in the areas we map. Three parts of the site touch data that relates to identifiable properties, and are worth being clear about.

  • Energy Performance Certificates describe individual buildings and are published as open data by the government, which operates its own opt-out process for householders who do not want their address included. We show certificates as they appear in the register at the time of our extract. If you have opted out since then, your certificate will disappear from our tools when we next rebuild. We do not publish postcodes with certificate records, and property points are randomly displaced by a small distance so that a point on the map is not an exact location.
  • Property sale prices come from the public Price Paid register and relate to transactions, not to named individuals. Price Paid Data does not identify buyers or sellers, and users of it have an obligation under the Open Government Licence to ensure their use does not breach the Data Protection Act 2018.
  • Survey microdata from the Living Costs and Food Survey and the National Travel Survey is used only to build synthetic households. No real household from either survey is published, and no output is traceable to a survey respondent.

For how this website handles data about you as a visitor, see the privacy notice.
